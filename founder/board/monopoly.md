# Monopoly lens
Score: 3 | Vote: PASS

## What is strong
- **The structure could compound.** A two-sided marketplace is one of the few small-business shapes that can build a real network effect: more venues bring more seekers, and more seekers bring more venues. Network effects in nightlife are local, though, so they have to be won one city at a time.
- **Its distribution choice suits the market.** Building on WhatsApp for confirmations and messaging puts the product in a channel the brief says both sides already use. Of everything here, this is the closest thing to know-how that is specific to South Africa.
- **Venues take little risk.** Starter has no fixed fee and charges commission only on bookings, so it is cheap to try.

## What would kill it (the top 3, most dangerous first)
1. **It starts too wide to win anywhere.** It launches in three cities and six venue types at once: clubs, bars, comedy, adventure, event spaces and restaurants. The Monopoly lens wants a big share of a small market first. Here a thin layer of supply gets spread across about 18 city-and-category cells, and in none of them is the app the obvious place to book. A marketplace with thin liquidity in every cell loses to whatever seekers use today.
2. **Nothing stops a copycat.** It names no proprietary technology and no secret. Booking, reminders, analytics and payouts are features that ticketing tools, reservation tools and the venues' own Instagram/WhatsApp setups can each copy. Venues can list on several platforms at once and pay nothing to leave. The 3% Starter commission tells us the pricing power is weak from day one. What does it hold in month six, let alone year ten?
3. **There is no distribution plan, and the date has slipped.** The page says nothing about how the first 100 venues and the first 1,000 seekers arrive, or what each one costs. The Q2 2026 launch has passed with no stated status, so we cannot answer "why now" either.

## The questions I would ask the founder
- Which single city, which single category and which neighbourhood would you dominate first, and what share of that cell's venues can you sign within 90 days?
- What do you know about SA nightlife or venue booking that incumbents do not? What is the secret?
- Who already sells booking or ticketing to these venues and seekers, and what do they charge? (/founder-competitors)
- What is the average booking value, and what does 3% or 8% of it actually earn per booking? How does a "booking" make money at a free-entry bar? (/founder-cfo)
- Do 21-35-year-olds actually lack a way to find and book these places, or is Instagram plus a WhatsApp message to the venue good enough? (/founder-consumer)
- How many venues and seekers are on the waitlist today, and what happened to the Q2 launch?
- Who is on the team, and has anyone sold to venues before?

## If FUND IF: the conditions
Does not apply, because the vote is PASS. Three things would reopen it. First, collapse the scope to one city and one category, for example comedy in one Johannesburg district. Second, show signed venue commitments that add up to a dominant share of that cell. Third, name a defensible wedge: exclusive inventory, a data advantage, or a workflow venues cannot easily replace. Until competitors and unit economics are mapped, this remains an undifferentiated booking app.
